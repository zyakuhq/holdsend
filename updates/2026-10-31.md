# Update: 2026-10-31

## What got done today

- The SMS hand-off in the notes example now survives a closed or killed page. The text and written time stay on the device, show up again on the next visit, and can be put back in the queue or marked as sent. A new browser test closes the page and checks it. ([PR 39](https://github.com/zyakuhq/holdsend/pull/39))
- CodeQL flagged storing the phone number, so the number is not stored. The person types it again after a reload. The docs say plainly that the library does not keep hand-off state for apps yet.

## Ready-to-post LinkedIn post

> Today I fixed a gap in the emergency part of HoldSend, our open-source engine for writing offline.
>
> When someone hands a message to SMS, it leaves the queue so it cannot also arrive by data. But if the page then closed before they pressed send in the messaging app, the text was gone. The example now keeps it on the device, shows it again on the next visit, and lets the person put it back.
>
> The lesson: a safety rule in one place (remove it from the queue) creates a duty in another (do not lose it). CodeQL also made me stop storing the phone number.
>
